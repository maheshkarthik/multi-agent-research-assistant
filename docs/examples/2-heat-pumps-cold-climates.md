# How well do heat pumps work in cold climates?

Heat pumps, particularly cold-climate models, perform effectively in cold climates with temperatures as low as -22°F (-30°C), though traditional air-source heat pumps face reduced efficiency below 40°F. Modern cold-climate heat pumps achieve higher efficiency ratings and can operate with integrated backup systems for extreme cold, while ground source heat pumps maintain superior performance due to stable ground temperatures.

## Challenges in cold climates

Traditional air-source heat pumps become less efficient when temperatures drop below 40°F, requiring auxiliary heating sources like electric resistance heating or gas furnaces [5]. Cold-climate heat pumps (CCHPs) operate with greater capacity and efficiency at outdoor temperatures below 32°F [1]. Most CCHPs include integrated electric resistance heaters for backup during extreme cold, though a properly sized system should not need backup above 5°F [3]. In areas with sustained temperatures below -10°F, an existing oil or gas furnace provides valuable redundancy [3].

## Performance comparisons

Modern cold-climate heat pumps can operate efficiently in temperatures as low as -13°F (-25°C) [6] and achieve HSPF ratings of 10-13 or higher, compared to standard heat pumps' 8-10 HSPF [6]. To meet ENERGY STAR Cold Climate standards, a heat pump must provide at least 70% of its rated heating capacity when the outdoor temperature is 5°F [8]. Cold Climate Air-Source Heat Pumps (ccASHPs) are designed to extract heat from air even when temperatures are below freezing [8]. However, heat pumps generally lose efficiency as outdoor temperatures drop, performing best in moderate climates [7].

## Maintenance and efficiency considerations

Ground source heat pumps have access to the thermal storage capacity of the ground, allowing them to produce more heat for less electricity in cold conditions compared to air source heat pumps [4]. In cold climates, ground-source heat pumps maintain higher efficiency than air-source heat pumps because the ground temperature remains stable even when air temperatures drop below freezing [12]. The highest efficiency heat pumps manufactured have a SEER rating of up to 24 [7], while ENERGY STAR requires a minimum SEER of 14 for heat pumps [7]. Proper maintenance, including regular filter changes and annual professional checks, costs $200–$400 per year but ensures longevity of 15–20 years [14].

## Conclusion

Cold-climate heat pumps are well-suited for cold climates with temperatures down to -22°F (-30°C), offering significant energy savings and efficiency improvements over traditional heating systems when properly maintained and sized. Ground source heat pumps provide the most consistent performance in extreme cold due to stable ground temperatures, while air source heat pumps require careful system design to avoid efficiency losses in freezing conditions.
