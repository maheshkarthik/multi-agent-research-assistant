# What are the health effects of intermittent fasting?

The health effects of intermittent fasting include potential improvements in cardiovascular and metabolic health, weight loss, and reduced insulin resistance in overweight or obese adults, though long-term sustainability and specific health benefits remain uncertain. Current evidence suggests limited support for heart health benefits and risks of adverse effects in certain populations, particularly those with diabetes. The research is ongoing and inconclusive for many outcomes.

## Primary health benefits of intermittent fasting

Intermittent fasting may improve cardiovascular and metabolic health in overweight or obese adults [2]. It may decrease insulin resistance and fasting insulin levels [2]. Additionally, it has been studied to reduce the risk of diet-related diseases such as metabolic syndrome [2]. However, the American Heart Association states there is no good evidence of heart health benefits from intermittent fasting [2]. The evidence for weight loss benefits is preliminary, with long-term sustainability of effects not yet established [2].

## Potential health risks and adverse effects

Intermittent fasting may cause low energy and hunger, particularly in individuals with insulin-dependent diabetes [2]. Adverse effects have not been comprehensively studied, leading some academics to classify it as a dietary fad [2]. The United States National Institute on Aging notes that current research is limited and inconclusive, and they do not recommend intermittent fasting to the general public [2]. There is little evidence to summarize the adverse events profile of intermittent fasting despite its widespread use in overweight or obese adults [9].

## Comparison of fasting protocols and health outcomes

Randomized clinical trials have compared various intermittent fasting protocols, including alternate day fasting, time-restricted eating, and whole day fasting [10]. A 16-hour fast provides modest metabolic benefits, while a 72-hour fast triggers deep autophagy and immune remodeling that shorter fasts cannot achieve [12]. The 16:8 fasting protocol and the 5:2 fasting protocol are among the major methods compared in evidence-based protocols for longevity [14]. However, long-term health benefits beyond weight loss remain unclear, with research indicating potential metabolic improvements but insufficient evidence for sustained effects [21].

## Conclusion

Intermittent fasting shows promise for improving metabolic health in overweight or obese adults but lacks robust evidence for long-term benefits and heart health. Adverse effects are not well understood, particularly for specific populations, and current guidelines do not recommend it as a general health strategy. More research is needed to clarify health outcomes and safety.
